The problems we keep meeting

Mortality you hear about too late

Birds start dying on Tuesday. The tally sheet reaches you on Friday. By then the problem has moved through the house and the loss is already done. On paper, a bad week looks exactly like a normal one until somebody sits down and adds it up.

Paper records that answer nothing

Exercise books can store numbers, but they cannot answer questions. What was the lay rate last week? Which batch is eating more than it produces? To find out, someone has to gather sheets and add columns by hand — so in practice, nobody does.

Feed and stock leaking quietly

Feed is your biggest cost, and bags have a way of disappearing between the store and the pen. Without daily records tying feed used to each batch, you cannot see the leak — you only feel it at the end of the month.

No numbers unless you drive there

If you are not physically on the farm, you are guessing. Sales happen, eggs move, stock shifts — and the only report is whatever a manager remembers to send. Owners tell us the same thing: "I don't really know how the farm did today."

What your system — and its agent — does about it

Your staff record the day on WhatsApp

No forms, no computer. A worker messages your farm's agent: "Sold 40 crates to Alhaji Musa at ₦6,500." The agent prepares the entry — 40 crates × ₦6,500 = ₦260,000, which batch, which date — and shows it for confirmation before anything saves. Staff can write in English, Hausa, or Pidgin.

Mortality flagged the same day

Deaths are logged per pen and per batch, every day, so a spike shows up the day it starts — not the week after. You see the trend over 7 and 14 days and can act while acting still matters.

Feed and stock, tracked to the bag

Feed used is recorded against each batch, so you finally see feed against eggs. The system flags low feed and unusual costs, and every sale carries a record of who logged it and who confirmed it.

Your morning report, compiled for you

Daily summaries come together automatically: eggs collected, lay rate, mortality, feed used, sales. Each batch carries a status — "On target" or "Watch" — so you know where to look first. You start the day with the numbers, not with phone calls.

Real build, running today

This is not a demo. UFMS — the Universal Farms Management System — runs every day at Universal Farms, our founder's own poultry operation in Kaduna. Eggs, lay rate, mortality, and feed are tracked per day, per house, per batch. Staff record everything by messaging the agent on WhatsApp, entries wait for a human confirmation, and the activity log shows who did what and when. We built it because our own farm needed it. Questions owners ask

Is there a poultry farm records app I can just download?

Ours is not a download, on purpose. Off-the-shelf apps force your farm into someone else's template — their pen structure, their reports, their workflow. We build your system around your farm: your pens, your batches, your people, your reports. Then your staff run it by messaging it on WhatsApp, which they already know how to use.

Can my farm workers use it if they are not good with technology?

If they can send a WhatsApp message, they can use it. There are no forms to learn. A worker types what happened in plain words — English, Hausa, Yoruba, Igbo, or Pidgin — and the agent prepares the record and asks them to confirm it. Training your people is part of every build, not an extra.
