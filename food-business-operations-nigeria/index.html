The problems we keep meeting

Order chaos at peak hour

Lunch rush hits and orders live in shouts, paper pads and WhatsApp screenshots. Some get made twice. Some never get made. By evening, nobody can say exactly what was sold.

Cash that never quite adds up

Card, transfer and cash all lumped into one figure. At closing, someone counts the drawer and hopes. When money goes missing, you find out weeks later — or never.

Vendors and stalls running on trust

Shared rent, daily settlements, outstanding balances — and each stall keeping its own notebook. You are owed money and cannot prove exactly how much.

No single view across outlets

Every evening you call each branch for their numbers. Figures arrive late, typed from memory, compiled by hand. You end up running the business on yesterday's guesses.

What your system — and its agent — does about it

One order flow, recorded as it happens

Every order enters the system the moment it's taken — at the counter, in the kitchen queue, or through a customer ordering app if your business needs one. Nothing lives in someone's head, and the day's sales build themselves as you trade.

Sales and expenses by WhatsApp message

Staff report to the assistant in plain language — English, Hausa, Yoruba, Igbo or Pidgin. It reads the message, shows a preview of the record, and saves only when they confirm. Every entry is logged with who sent it and when.

Vendor and stall coordination, per profile

Each vendor or stall gets its own profile: their orders, their sales, their rent and settlements, their outstanding balance. They see only their own numbers. You see everyone's.

Closing numbers on your phone

At the end of the day you get the closing picture on WhatsApp: sales broken out by card, transfer and cash, expenses paid, and anything unreconciled flagged. The day doesn't close until the money adds up.

Real build, running today

This is not theory. We built TruckVille OS, the operations system behind TruckVille, a real food-truck venue in Abuja. More than 200 orders a day flow through it: live orders and transactions, payment methods broken out so reconciliation is not a guessing game, a profile and queue for every vendor with rent and settlements tracked, and an end-of-day closeout that will not close until the money adds up. Its customer-facing half, the TruckVille ordering app, lets guests order from any truck in one cart and pay once — and every order lands straight in the venue's books, not in a second system to reconcile later. Is there a POS alternative for a food business in Nigeria?

A POS records what happens at the counter. What we build is the operations system around it: order flow, sales and expense records, vendor settlements, and a daily closing that has to balance before the day ends. If you need order capture, we build that in too — the venue system we built in Abuja handles over 200 orders a day. It replaces the pile of notebooks and separate apps, not just the cash register.
